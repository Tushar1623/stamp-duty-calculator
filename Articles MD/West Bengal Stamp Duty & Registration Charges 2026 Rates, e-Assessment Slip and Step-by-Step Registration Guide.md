# West Bengal Stamp Duty Calculator 2026: Rates, Circle Rate Check, e-Assessment Slip and GRIPS Payment Guide

**Last reviewed: October 2026 | Reading time: about 11 minutes**

A buyer signs for a ₹80 Lakh flat in Kolkata. A few days later the e-Assessment slip arrives, and the registration cost is ₹1.6 Lakhs higher than the cost sheet the builder handed over. The sheet was quoting pre-July-2024 rates, and nobody at the sales office mentioned it.

Mistakes like this are common in West Bengal. Many brochures and bank pages still quote the old rebated rates, the state's own system shows a different number from the one buyers expect, and one wrong assumption about the property's location can stall a registration.

This guide explains what you will actually pay, how the figure is arrived at, and how to complete the process online without surprises.

> **Quick answer (2026):** In West Bengal, stamp duty on a property purchase is **6% up to ₹1 Crore and 7% above ₹1 Crore** in municipal and corporation areas such as Kolkata and Howrah. In panchayat (rural) areas it is **5% and 6%**. Add a **registration fee of about 1%**. Men and women pay the same. The Covid-era 2% rebate ended on 30 June 2024. Duty is charged on the **higher** of the agreement value and the state's assessed market value.

## 1. What Stamp Duty and Registration Fee Actually Are

These are two separate charges, and buyers often lump them together.

**Stamp duty** is a tax on the document. Under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, as amended for West Bengal, a conveyance (sale deed) is not valid evidence of transfer unless the required duty has been paid. The state sets the rate.

**Registration fee** is the charge for recording the deed in the public register under the Registration Act, 1908. Registration gives your ownership a public, dated, searchable record, which matters for loans, resale and any later dispute.

Both are paid to the state government before or at the time of registration. Neither can be paid in instalments, and neither is refundable simply because a deal falls through, except in narrow refund cases covered in the state's rules.

**Governing law.** Duty on a sale deed is set under Article 23 of Schedule 1A of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, as amended for West Bengal. The state's published schedule sets 6% of market value in municipal, corporation and notified areas and 5% in panchayat areas, with an additional 1% in both once the market value exceeds ₹1 Crore. Registration is governed by the Registration Act, 1908, and the Directorate of Registration and Stamp Revenue administers both.

## 2. Agreement Value vs Market Value (Circle Rate)

This is the concept that surprises most buyers.

- **Agreement value** is the price you and the seller agreed on.
- **Market value** is the floor value the state assigns to the property, based on location, land use, road, building type and similar factors. Buyers and portals often call it the **circle rate** or ready reckoner rate. West Bengal's registration portal calls it market value.

**Stamp duty is charged on whichever is higher.** If you buy a flat for ₹45 Lakhs but the state's assessed market value is ₹60 Lakhs, you pay duty on ₹60 Lakhs.

West Bengal does not hand you one printed rate per street. The portal computes market value from the details you enter (district, mouza, road, use of property, and for flats, built-up area and age). So the only reliable number is the one on your **e-Assessment slip** (explained below).

**A note on "IGR West Bengal circle rate".** Many buyers search for it, "IGR" being the short form of the Inspector General of Registration, who heads the state's registration directorate. In West Bengal the registration directorate's portal shows the circle rate as the market value of your specific property, and the West Bengal stamp duty calculator on that portal computes duty, fee and market value together. Use it for an early figure, then confirm against your e-Assessment slip.

> **Practical rule:** Budget on the higher of your price and the portal's market value, never on the price alone.

## 3. West Bengal Stamp Duty Rates in 2026

| Property location | Value up to ₹1 Crore | Value above ₹1 Crore | Registration fee |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kolkata, Howrah and other corporation / municipal areas | 6% | 7% | About 1% |
| Panchayat (rural) areas | 5% | 6% | About 1% |

A few points that matter:

- **The same rates apply to flats, houses and plots.** The type of property does not change the slab; location and value do.
- **Check the jurisdiction, not the postal address.** A plot on Kolkata's fringe may still lie in a gram panchayat, which saves a full percentage point. Equally, do not assume panchayat rates apply until the portal confirms them. Newer planned areas such as New Town fall under their own development authority, so confirm the classification on the portal rather than assuming.
- **The ₹1 Crore step can be steep.** Many calculators apply the higher rate to the *whole* value once you cross ₹1 Crore, not only to the excess. If your deed is assessed that way, ₹1,00,00,000 attracts ₹6,00,000 duty at 6%, while ₹1,01,00,000 attracts ₹7,07,000 at 7%. That is ₹1 Lakh more in value but ₹1.07 Lakh more in duty. In effect the state charges one extra percentage point above ₹1 Crore (7% instead of 6% in urban areas, 6% instead of 5% in rural areas); it is a higher slab, not a separate surcharge. Confirm on the slip.
- **Registration fee varies slightly between sources.** Most show 1%; some show 1.1%. The e-Assessment slip is final.

## 4. Step-by-Step Online Registration Process

West Bengal's registration process runs largely online through the official portal, **wbregistration.gov.in**.

**Step 1: Get an early estimate.** Use the portal's calculator for market value, stamp duty and registration fee. Choose the right option (land, land with structure, or apartment) and enter district, mouza and property details.

**Step 2: Fill the e-Requisition.** This is the online data-entry form where you enter details of the property, the buyer(s) and seller(s), and the transaction. Accuracy matters here. Name spellings and property descriptions should match your ID and title documents exactly.

**Step 3: Receive the e-Assessment slip.** The system generates a slip showing the assessed value, duty and fee payable. It carries a **14-digit query number and query year**. Keep both safe. This slip is your official figure.

**Step 4: Pay stamp duty through the GRIPS portal.** Payment is made via GRIPS, the state's payment gateway, using net banking, SBI ePay, debit card or UPI. As per current guidance, online payment is permitted once duty exceeds ₹5,000 and is compulsory at ₹5 Lakhs or more. Save the payment receipt and challan.

**Step 5: Book your registration slot and appear.** Book the appointment at the relevant Registrar/ADSR office. Buyer, seller and witnesses attend with originals of the documents listed in Section 7. The registering officer verifies identity, takes biometrics and photographs, and registers the deed.

**Step 6: Collect the registered deed and start mutation.** Once registered, you can proceed to mutation (see Section 9).

> **Tip:** Do the e-Requisition *after* you have a near-final draft deed. Errors in the property description or party details often mean re-entering the form and sometimes re-assessment.

## 5. Concessions, Exemptions and the Rebate That Ended

### The 2% rebate has ended

In 2021, to revive sales after the pandemic, West Bengal temporarily cut stamp duty by 2 percentage points and reduced assessed market values by 10%. The relief was extended several times, the last time to **30 June 2024**, and the government withdrew it from **1 July 2024**. It has not been reinstated.

Many builder brochures, bank pages and older blogs still show 4% (municipal) or 3% (panchayat). Any cost sheet that predates July 2024 will understate your cost by about 2 percentage points of the value.

### No concession for women buyers

Unlike several other states, West Bengal does not currently offer a lower stamp duty rate for women. A woman buying a ₹60 Lakh flat in Kolkata pays the same 6% duty and \~1% fee as a man. Some calculators still show a women's concession; do not rely on those.

### Gifts and partitions within the family

Transfers within close family carry a much lower rate:

- **Gift deed** to a parent, child, spouse, sibling or other lineal relative: **0.5%** stamp duty on market value, plus the registration fee of about 1%.
- **Gift to anyone else** (nephew, friend, and so on): charged at the full conveyance rate for the area.
- **Partition deed:** 0.5% on the market value of the separated share(s), plus the registration fee.

Notice the quirk: on a family gift, the **registration fee is larger than the stamp duty**.

### Other relief

Specific exemptions and reduced rates may exist for particular instruments or notified categories. Always ask the registering office or check the latest notification before assuming any other concession applies to you.

## 6A. Other Documents, Concessions and Tax Savings

A brief outline of the deeds buyers most often sign alongside a sale. Separate guides cover each in depth.

- **Gift deed (family).** Article 33 sets 0.5% of market value for a gift to a member of the donor's family. A gift to anyone else pays the conveyance rate. The registration fee of about 1% applies either way.
- **Partition deed.** Article 45 sets 0.5% of the market value of the separated share or shares.
- **Power of attorney.** A PoA to a promoter or developer to construct on, sell or transfer immovable property carries fixed duty by the property's market value: ₹5,000 (up to ₹30 Lakhs), ₹7,000 (₹30 to ₹60 Lakhs), ₹10,000 (₹60 Lakhs to ₹1 Crore), ₹20,000 (₹1 to ₹1.5 Crore), ₹40,000 (₹1.5 to ₹3 Crore) and ₹75,000 (above ₹3 Crore).
- **Lease.** For a fixed-rent lease with no premium, duty uses the conveyance rate on a multiple of the average annual rent: twice the rent for terms above one and up to ten years, three times for terms above ten and up to thirty years. Beyond thirty years, it is charged on the market value. Confirm the treatment of your lease on the slip.

**Section 80C.** A buyer of a residential property can claim stamp duty and registration charges as a deduction within the ₹1.5 Lakh overall limit in the year of payment, under the old tax regime. The property should not be sold within five years, or the deduction can be reversed. Income-tax law was reorganised in 2026, so confirm the section reference on the income-tax portal before filing.

## 7. Documents Checklist

Requirements can vary by deal type and registrar office, so use this as a baseline and confirm locally.

**Identity and tax**

- PAN card of buyer and seller
- Aadhaar (or other valid photo ID) of all parties and witnesses
- Passport, visa/OCI details and overseas address proof for NRI parties

**Property and title**

- Draft sale deed (or conveyance deed)
- Agreement for sale, if one exists
- Previous title deeds showing the chain of ownership
- Mutation record and latest municipal property tax receipt
- Society/Apartment Owners' Association NOC, where applicable
- Builder documents for new flats: allotment letter, completion/occupancy papers, sanctioned plan
- Land records for plots (Banglarbhumi extracts such as khatian and porcha, plus dag/plot details)

**Payment and tax**

- GRIPS payment receipt and e-Assessment slip
- TDS proof: where the consideration is ₹50 Lakhs or more, the buyer generally deducts 1% TDS on payment to a resident seller and files Form 26QB. Income-tax rules were reorganised in 2026, so confirm the current form and section references on the income-tax portal. If the seller is an NRI, TDS rules differ and are considerably heavier; take professional advice.

**On the day**

- Two witnesses with ID
- Passport-size photographs as required
- Power of attorney, if anyone is appearing through an attorney

## 8. Common Pitfalls That Cause Delays or Penalties

1. **Using an old cost sheet.** Anything quoting 4% or 5% for municipal areas predates the July 2024 withdrawal.
2. **Budgeting on price instead of market value.** If the assessed value is higher, the duty follows it.
3. **Under-valuing the deed.** Registrars can refer a deed for valuation if the declared value looks low. The result can be a demand for the shortfall plus penalties and delay.
4. **Splitting the deal to dodge the ₹1 Crore step.** Putting parking or fittings into a separate agreement only helps if the assessed market value is also under ₹1 Crore. A registrar who sees an artificial split can reassess the whole transaction.
5. **Wrong jurisdiction.** Assuming panchayat rates when the property is municipal leads to underpayment and a delayed deed.
6. **Name or description mismatches.** The deed, e-Requisition, ID and title documents must agree character for character.
7. **Missing TDS compliance.** Skipping the 1% deduction and challan creates tax trouble for the buyer later.
8. **Assuming registration equals mutation.** Registration does not change the municipal or land record; mutation is a separate step.
9. **Relying on an unverified women's concession.** There isn't one currently in West Bengal.

## 9. After Registration: Mutation and Property Tax

Registering the deed does not update the municipal records, so your property tax bill still carries the seller's name until you complete mutation.

West Bengal has linked the two steps through **e-Nathikaran**. Your registered deed data flows to the urban services mutation portal, so when you log in there you should find an application already started, marked as originating from e-Nathikaran. You verify the details, upload any requested documents, pay the demand notice online and download the mutation certificate. Mutation fees are set by each municipality, usually on the property's annual value.

For agricultural land and rural plots, the land-records route applies instead, and the record is updated through the state land records system.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the stamp duty in West Bengal in 2026?

In municipal and corporation areas, stamp duty is 6% for property up to ₹1 Crore and 7% above that. In panchayat areas it is 5% up to ₹1 Crore and 6% above. A registration fee of about 1% is added in both cases. The duty is calculated on the higher of the agreement value and the assessed market value.

### Is there a stamp duty concession for women buyers in West Bengal?

No. Current guidance shows no gender-based concession, so women and men pay the same rates. Calculators that still show a lower rate for women appear to be outdated.

### Is the 2% stamp duty rebate still available?

No. The 2% stamp duty reduction and 10% market-value reduction introduced in 2021 were last extended to 30 June 2024 and withdrawn from 1 July 2024. Full rates apply now.

### How do I find the market value (circle rate) of my property?

Use the market value calculator on wbregistration.gov.in, choosing the correct option for land, land with structure, or apartment, and enter the district, mouza, road and property details. Then complete the e-Requisition to get your e-Assessment slip, which carries the exact value, duty and fee.

### Can I pay West Bengal stamp duty online?

Yes. Payment is made through GRIPS by net banking, SBI ePay, debit card or UPI. Online payment is available once duty exceeds ₹5,000 and is compulsory at ₹5 Lakhs and above. Payment must be completed before registration; it cannot be paid in instalments.

### How much does a gift deed to a family member cost?

For a gift to a parent, child, spouse, sibling or other lineal relative, stamp duty is 0.5% of market value plus a registration fee of about 1%. On a ₹60 Lakh property that is ₹30,000 plus ₹60,000, a total of ₹90,000. A gift to anyone outside that circle is charged at the full conveyance rate.

### Do I need to do anything after the deed is registered?

Yes. You should apply for mutation so the property tax record moves to your name. In most municipalities the process now starts from your registration data through e-Nathikaran, and you complete and pay for it on the urban services portal.
