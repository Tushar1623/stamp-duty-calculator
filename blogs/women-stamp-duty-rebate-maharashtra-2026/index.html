Women Stamp Duty Rebate in Maharashtra 2026: Eligibility, Sole Ownership Rule and Joint Purchases

📅 Last Reviewed: October 2026
⏱️ Reading Time: 10 min read
🏛️ Jurisdiction: Maharashtra

Maharashtra gives women a 1% reduction in stamp duty on residential property, but only when the woman is the sole owner, so a joint purchase with a male co-owner generally forfeits it. The rebate has no cap on the property value, needs no separate application, and in 2026 sources report that the old 15-year resale condition has been removed.

A couple buys a ₹1.2 Crore flat in Pune. Registered in the husband's name, the stamp duty is 7%. Registered in the wife's name alone, it is 6%, which is ₹1.2 Lakh less. Registered in both names, it is not 6%. Many buyers learn this at the Sub-Registrar's office, when the figure on the challan is not the one the broker promised.

This guide sets out who qualifies for the women's concession, what "sole ownership" means, how joint purchases are treated, and what to think about before choosing whose name goes on the deed.

Quick answer (2026): Women buying residential property in their sole name (or, per several sources, only in the names of women) get a 1% reduction in stamp duty anywhere in Maharashtra. A joint purchase with a male co-owner generally pays the male rate, although a few calculators show a blended rate. The concession does not apply to commercial property. The registration fee (1%, capped at ₹30,000) is not reduced.

1. Where the Concession Comes From

Stamp duty in Maharashtra is levied under the Maharashtra Stamp Act, 1958. The women's concession is a 1% reduction in the base duty on residential property registered in a woman's name. It was introduced to encourage women's ownership of property, and sources link it to the state's Rajmata Jijau Griha Swamini Yojana, reported as effective from 1 April 2021.

When the concession began, women who used it were required to hold the property for 15 years or repay the difference if they sold earlier. 2026 sources report that this lock-in has been removed. Confirm the current position with the Sub-Registrar before you buy, because it is a condition that has changed once already.

The concession is applied at registration. There is no separate application, and no cap on the value of the property.

2. What the Rebate Is Worth, City by City

The concession takes 1% off the base duty, so the woman's rate is always one point below the male rate in the same city.

City Male buyer Woman, sole owner Saving
Mumbai (BMC) 6% 5% 1% of value
Pune, Pimpri-Chinchwad 7% 6% 1% of value
Thane 7% 6% 1% of value
Navi Mumbai (most sources) 7% 6% 1% of value

The metro cess and the local body tax stay in place for women. Only the base duty falls from 5% to 4%. A separate guide in this series explains how the headline rates are built.

3. Eligibility: The Rules in Plain Terms

Three conditions recur across the sources.

  • Residential property only. Flats, houses and residential plots qualify. Shops, offices and industrial property do not.
  • A woman's name only. The property must be registered in the woman's sole name. Several sources extend this to a registration in the names of more than one woman, such as a mother and daughter.
  • Maharashtra property. The concession applies to property in the state, across all cities.

What the concession does not require:

  • No income limit and no age limit.
  • No cap on the property value. A ₹5 Crore flat qualifies as much as a ₹50 Lakh one.
  • No separate form. The registrar applies the rate to the category of buyer.

What it does not change:

  • The registration fee. It stays at 1%, capped at ₹30,000.
  • The Ready Reckoner basis. Duty is still charged on the higher of your price and the Ready Reckoner value.

4. Ownership Structures Compared

Ownership Concession? Rate in Pune (illustration)
Woman, sole owner Yes 6%
Two or more women, no male owner Yes, per several sources 6%
Man, sole owner No 7%
Man and man No 7%
Man and woman, joint Generally no 7%, or a blended 6.5% in some calculators
Woman as first applicant, man as co-owner Disputed One source says yes, most say no

The last two rows are where buyers get caught. Sources contradict each other, and some Pune guides show a blended 6.5% for a male-female joint purchase. The rule that most sources agree on is the simple one: if a man is on the deed as an owner, plan for the male rate.

5. Why Joint Male-Female Buying Forfeits the Rebate

The concession is framed as a benefit for property held by women. It is not a discount on a woman's share of a jointly held property. When a man is an owner, the registrar charges the standard rate on the transaction, because the property is not held solely by a woman.

The consequence is counter-intuitive. A couple that buys jointly, which is often the legally and emotionally natural choice, pays more than a couple that registers the wife alone. In Pune on a ₹1.2 Crore flat, that is ₹1.2 Lakh.

Two points of caution.

  • "Blended" rates are not a reliable plan. Where a calculator shows 6.5% for a joint purchase, there is no guarantee the registrar will apply it. Budget for the male rate, and treat any lower figure as a bonus you confirm in advance.
  • First applicant does not settle it. A claim that being the first-named applicant is enough appears in one source and is contradicted by others.

6. Before You Register in a Woman's Name

The stamp duty saving is real, but it is one part of a larger decision. A property registered solely in a woman's name belongs to her. Think about the following before you choose, and take advice for your situation.

  • Who pays. If the husband funds the purchase, there can be income-tax consequences. Income from a property can be clubbed with the income of the person who transferred the money, and rules on gifts between spouses apply. A tax adviser can tell you how this applies to your funding.
  • Home loan. Lenders generally expect co-borrowers to be co-owners. A male co-borrower who is not on the title may not be acceptable to every lender, so check before you decide.
  • Title and succession. Sole ownership affects who inherits and what happens if the relationship changes.
  • Source of funds. Keep a clean record of how the purchase was funded.

These are not reasons to skip the concession. They are reasons to decide on whose name goes on the deed for more than the stamp duty.

7. Step-by-Step: Claiming the Concession

Step 1: Confirm the property is residential and that its use is shown as residential in the agreement.

Step 2: Decide the ownership structure and make sure the draft agreement and deed name the woman as the sole owner, or all owners as women.

Step 3: Look up the Ready Reckoner value on igrmaharashtra.gov.in and compare it with your price.

Step 4: Compute duty at the woman's rate, and add the registration fee.

Step 5: Pay through GRAS at gras.mahakosh.gov.in and keep the challan.

Step 6: Appear at the Sub-Registrar's office with the original documents, ID and two witnesses.

Before you pay: Run both versions through the StampDutyCalc.in calculator: the woman as sole owner and the joint registration. The gap between them is the price of adding a co-owner to the deed.

9. Other Documents and Tax Savings

This is a brief outline. Separate guides cover each deed.

  • Gift deed. A gift of residential or agricultural property to a spouse, child, grandchild or a deceased son's widow is reported at a flat ₹200 under Article 34. This is a separate route to put property in a woman's name, with different consequences from a purchase.
  • Power of attorney. A PoA authorising sale of immovable property is taxed under Article 48 at the conveyance rate on market value. A PoA without consideration to close relatives is reported at ₹500.
  • Leave and license. 0.25% of the rent for the period plus a notional amount for the refundable deposit, with a ₹1,000 registration fee in urban areas.

Tax savings. Stamp duty and registration charges on a residential purchase qualify within the ₹1.5 Lakh limit in the year of payment, under the old tax regime. For payments up to 31 March 2026 the section is 80C of the Income-tax Act, 1961. From 1 April 2026 it is Section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Joint owners can claim their own share of the payment, so a sole woman owner claims it in full.

10. Documents Checklist and Common Errors

For the Sub-Registrar appointment

  • PAN and Aadhaar of the woman buyer and the seller
  • Agreement for sale or sale deed naming the woman as sole owner, and previous title documents
  • Society NOC and share certificate for resale flats
  • For new flats: RERA number and occupancy documents
  • GRAS challan or e-SBTR, and the valuation printout
  • TDS proof where the consideration is ₹50 Lakhs or more (Form 26QB; confirm current form references under the Income-tax Act, 2025)
  • Proof of funds, if the purchase is funded by someone else
  • Two witnesses with ID and photographs

Common errors

  1. Adding a male co-owner and assuming the concession survives.
  2. Relying on a "blended" joint rate seen on a calculator.
  3. Claiming the concession on a shop or office.
  4. Forgetting that the registration fee is not reduced.
  5. Choosing sole ownership for the duty saving without checking the home loan, tax and succession position.
  6. Assuming the old 15-year resale condition still applies, or that it has gone, without confirming.
  7. Entering the buyer category wrongly when computing the figure.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

Do women get a stamp duty concession in Maharashtra?

Yes. Women buying residential property in their sole name get a 1% reduction in stamp duty, in every city. It does not apply to commercial property.

Does a joint purchase with my husband get the women's rebate?

Generally no. Most sources say the concession applies only when the property is registered solely in a woman's name, or in the names of women only. Some calculators show a blended rate, so confirm with the registrar.

Is there a resale lock-in on the women's concession?

An earlier condition required holding the property for 15 years or repaying the concession. 2026 sources report that it has been removed. Confirm the current position before you buy.

Is the registration fee reduced for women?

No. The fee stays at 1% of the value, capped at ₹30,000 above ₹30 Lakhs.

How much does the women's rebate save?

1% of the property value. That is ₹45,000 on a ₹45 Lakh flat and ₹1.2 Lakh on a ₹1.2 Crore flat.

Can a mother and daughter buy together and keep the rebate?

Several sources say yes, when all the owners are women. Confirm with the Sub-Registrar.
