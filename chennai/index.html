Complete Guide to Chennai & Tamil Nadu Stamp Duty (2025–2026)

Everything you need to know about TNREGINET guideline values, women buyer concessions, family settlement deeds, and Sub-Registrar Office procedures in Chennai.

1. Statutory Tariff Structure for Chennai Property Registrations

Property registrations in Tamil Nadu are administered by the Registration Department (TNREGINET) under the provisions of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (Tamil Nadu Amendments) and the Registration Act, 1908. Unlike states with tiered slabs, Tamil Nadu levies a straightforward, flat tariff on standard residential conveyancing instruments:

  • Stamp Duty: 7% of the higher of agreement consideration or guideline value.
  • Registration Fee: 4% of the higher valuation.
  • Total Combined Tariff: 11% net statutory outgo.

2. Guideline Value vs. Agreement Value: The Higher Valuation Rule

Under Section 47-A of the Indian Stamp Act (Tamil Nadu Amendment), statutory charges are charged on whichever is greater:

  1. Actual Agreement Value: The gross financial consideration agreed between the seller and purchaser in the contract.
  2. TNREGINET Guideline Value: The government's notified baseline circle valuation for that specific survey number, street, or layout.

For example, if you purchase an apartment in OMR (Thoraipakkam) for ₹80 Lakhs, but the guideline value assesses the undivided share of land (UDS) and built-up area at ₹85 Lakhs, stamp duty (7%) and registration fees (4%) are levied on the ₹85 Lakhs figure.

3. Concessions & Demographic Provisions

Sole Female Buyer Concession

In Tamil Nadu, the 7% base stamp duty applies equally across all genders. However, the state provides a 1% concession on the registration fee (reducing it from 4% to 3%), bringing total outgo to 10%. This concession is subject to strict eligibility limits: it applies only to transactions where the total property valuation does not exceed ₹10 Lakhs and the title is registered in the sole name of a female purchaser.

Family Gift and Settlement Deeds (Statutory Caps)

When transferring immovable property among close bloodline family members (Father, Mother, Spouse, Son, Daughter, Grandchildren, Brother, Sister) without financial consideration, the state grants significant relief:

  • Concessional Stamp Duty: 1% of property value, strictly capped at a maximum of ₹40,000.
  • Concessional Registration Fee: 1% of property value, strictly capped at a maximum of ₹10,000.
  • Maximum Government Charges: Regardless of whether the property is worth ₹1 Crore or ₹10 Crores, the total statutory fee cannot exceed ₹50,000.

4. Chennai Micro-Market Overview

Chennai's residential real estate landscape features diverse valuation bands across prime IT corridors and established central localities:

  • OMR (Old Mahabalipuram Road): Taramani, Perungudi, Thoraipakkam, Sholinganallur, and Navalur. High concentration of gated communities with substantial UDS allocations.
  • ECR (East Coast Road): Thiruvanmiyur, Palavakkam, Kottivakkam, Neelankarai. Premium coastal residential and villa plots with higher guideline baselines.
  • Central Chennai: Anna Nagar, T. Nagar, Alwarpet, Mylapore, Adyar, Besant Nagar. Established residential hubs with premium guideline values per square foot.
  • Suburban Corridors: Tambaram, Chromepet, Porur, Medavakkam, Guindy, Pallavaram. Popular among first-time home buyers.
