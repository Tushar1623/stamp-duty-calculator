1. Overview: Property Registration in West Bengal

In West Bengal, the registration of immovable property (apartments, residential plots, commercial spaces, and land parcels) is governed by the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (as amended by West Bengal Stamp Amendment Acts) and the Registration Act, 1908. The administration is managed digitally by the Directorate of Registration and Stamp Revenue (IGR West Bengal).

Whenever a property transaction occurs, two distinct statutory levies must be paid to the state exchequer before the Sub-Registrar executes the deed:

  • Stamp Duty: A state tax levied on the transaction value or the government circle rate (market value), whichever is higher.
  • Registration Fee: A statutory processing fee charged to officially record the title transfer in the sub-registry records and generate the registered deed document.

2. Current Stamp Duty Rate Slabs (Urban vs. Rural)

Stamp duty in West Bengal is primarily differentiated based on whether the property falls under an Urban Area (Municipal Corporation, Municipality, or Notified Area Authority like Kolkata Municipal Corporation, Howrah Municipal Corporation, Bidhannagar, NKDA New Town) or a Rural Area (Gram Panchayat jurisdiction).

Property Location Market Value Threshold Stamp Duty Rate Registration Fee Total Statutory Outgo
Urban Area (Corporations, Municipalities, NKDA) Up to ₹1,00,00,000 (₹1 Crore) 6.0% 1.1% 7.1%
Urban Area (High-Value) Above ₹1,00,00,000 (> ₹1 Crore) 7.0% (6% + 1% Surcharge) 1.1% 8.1%
Rural Area (Gram Panchayat) Up to ₹1,00,00,000 (₹1 Crore) 5.0% 1.1% 6.1%
Rural Area (High-Value) Above ₹1,00,00,000 (> ₹1 Crore) 6.0% (5% + 1% Surcharge) 1.1% 7.1%
💡 Note on Temporary Rebates: During the pandemic and post-pandemic period, West Bengal provided a temporary 2% stamp duty rebate and 10% circle rate reduction. That temporary relief scheme concluded on 30 June 2024. All calculations since 1 July 2024 and through 2026 operate under standard statutory rates (6% Urban, 5% Rural).

3. The High-Value Surcharge (> ₹1 Crore)

Under the West Bengal Stamp Act, when the determined market value of a property exceeds ₹1,00,00,000 (Rupees One Crore), a 1% additional surcharge applies to the total stamp duty assessment.

This means:

  • An urban apartment valued at ₹95 Lakhs is taxed at 6% stamp duty.
  • An urban apartment valued at ₹1.10 Crores is taxed at 7% stamp duty across its full valuation.

4. Concessions for Women Buyers and Gift Deeds

A. Women Buyer Concession

To encourage female homeownership, the Government of West Bengal offers a 1% rebate on stamp duty when a residential property is purchased solely in the name of a female buyer (or as primary woman owner). This brings the effective stamp duty down to:

  • 5% for properties up to ₹1 Crore in urban areas (instead of 6%).
  • 4% for properties up to ₹1 Crore in rural areas (instead of 5%).

B. Blood-Relative Gift Deeds (0.5% Flat Duty)

Transferring property to immediate family members (spouse, parents, children, grandchildren, siblings) via a registered Gift Deed enjoys significant statutory relief:

  • Stamp Duty: Flat 0.5% of the property's circle rate value.
  • Exemption: Strictly exempt from the 1% high-value surcharge even if the property value exceeds ₹1 Crore.
  • Registration Fee: Flat 1.1% on market value.

C. General Power of Attorney (GPA)

A General Power of Attorney without consideration is assessed at a flat nominal fee of ₹1,000 stamp duty and ₹200 registration fee.

5. Registration Fee Structure (Flat 1.1%)

Unlike some states where registration fees are stepped or capped at a maximum limit (e.g. Maharashtra's ₹30,000 cap), West Bengal charges a flat 1.1% registration fee on the higher of the agreement value or government circle rate across all residential sale transactions without an upper ceiling.

6. Step-by-Step Calculation Examples

Example 1: Urban Flat in Kolkata / New Town (₹50 Lakhs)

  • Property Valuation: ₹50,00,000
  • Zone: Urban (KMC / NKDA)
  • Buyer: Male / General
  • Stamp Duty (6%): ₹3,00,000
  • Registration Fee (1.1%): ₹55,000
  • Total Statutory Fee: ₹3,55,000 (7.1%)

Example 2: High-Value Flat in South Kolkata (₹1.5 Crores)

  • Property Valuation: ₹1,50,00,000
  • Zone: Urban
  • Stamp Duty (7% - including 1% surcharge): ₹10,50,000
  • Registration Fee (1.1%): ₹1,65,000
  • Total Statutory Fee: ₹12,15,000 (8.1%)

Example 3: Family Gift Deed in Howrah (₹80 Lakhs)

  • Property Valuation: ₹80,00,000
  • Deed Type: Gift to Son / Daughter (Blood Relative)
  • Concessional Stamp Duty (0.5%): ₹40,000
  • Registration Fee (1.1%): ₹88,000
  • Total Statutory Fee: ₹1,28,000 (1.6%)

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Calculating only on the Agreement Value: In West Bengal, if the official circle rate (government market value) is higher than the agreement value stated in your deed, duty is calculated strictly on the higher circle rate value.
  2. Forgetting 1% TDS (Section 194-IA): If the total consideration or market value equals or exceeds ₹50 Lakhs, the buyer must deduct 1% TDS and deposit it via Form 26QB before registration.
  3. Missing Surcharge on ₹1Cr+: Ensure you budget for the full 7% stamp duty if the valuation crosses ₹1,00,00,001.
