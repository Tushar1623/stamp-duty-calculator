Common Questions

Stamp Duty FAQ

As of 2025, Meghalaya (9.9%), Mizoram (9%), Nagaland (8.25%), Assam (8.25% for male buyers), Kerala (8%), and Puducherry (8%) have among the highest stamp duty rates in India. Maharashtra's effective rate is 6% for male buyers (5% stamp duty + 1% metro cess), which is moderate. Gujarat at 4.9% and Goa at 3.5% are among the lowest for larger cities.
Goa has one of the lowest stamp duty rates at 3.5%, making it attractive for second-home purchases. Gujarat charges 4.9% (3.9% for female buyers), Karnataka charges 0% on properties below ₹20 lakh, and Jharkhand charges 4%. Karnataka's slab system is particularly attractive for affordable housing — properties under ₹20L face nil stamp duty.
Stamp duty is the primary tax levied on the transfer of property — it forms the bulk of the government outgo. Registration charge is a separate fee paid to the sub-registrar's office for recording the transaction in government records. Without registration, a property transfer is not legally enforceable. In Maharashtra, registration is capped at ₹30,000 regardless of property value, while most other states charge 1–2% of the property value as registration fee.
Yes. Stamp duty applies on all property transfers — new, resale, gift deeds, and inheritance partitions. The rate is the same whether you buy directly from a developer or from a previous owner. For resale properties, the stamp duty basis is the higher of the agreement value or the state government's circle rate / ready reckoner rate for that area.
Yes, in most states stamp duty is refundable if the property deal is cancelled — but typically with a deduction of 10–25% as administrative charges. The refund claim must usually be filed within 6 months to 2 years of payment, depending on the state. In Maharashtra, refund applications go to the Collector of Stamps. The process can take several months to process.
AP and Telangana levy a Transfer Duty (TD) in addition to Stamp Duty. In Telangana: Stamp Duty 4% + Transfer Duty 1.5% + Registration 0.5% = 6% total. In AP: Stamp Duty 5% + Transfer Duty 1.5% + Registration 0.5% = 7% total. This dual levy makes these states comparable in total cost to Maharashtra despite the lower headline SD rate.
Yes. Under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, stamp duty and registration charges paid for purchasing a residential property are deductible up to ₹1.5 lakh in the year of payment. This applies only to residential properties (not commercial), and only in the year the payment is actually made. If your total 80C investments (PF, ELSS, PPF, etc.) already exceed ₹1.5L, the stamp duty deduction provides no additional benefit.
